## Why production showed 636 fresh of 4515

Search and `GET /api/jobs/count` treat a job as fresh only when `posted_at` is within 45 days (`maxAgeDays`). The other rows stay in the table. Nothing was deleting a posting after it left the employer's board, and nothing was deleting rows once they aged past 45 days, so `total` is every row ever upserted.

The crawlers store the employer's own date (`updated_at`, `createdAt`, `published_at`), not the time of the crawl. A role that is still open but was posted 46 or more days ago counts in `total` and is hidden from search. That is why a daily crawl can succeed and fresh can still be a small slice of total.

The scheduled crawl does run in this process: `node-cron` in `server.js` starts a quick crawl every 6 hours and a full crawl at 02:00 UTC, and Railway's start command is `npm start`, so the cron is inside the web process. Two gaps kept the index thin:

1. The Greenhouse 'all companies' crawl reads `https://boards.greenhouse.io/sitemap.xml`. On 17 Nov 2026 that URL 301s to a 404, so the full crawl discovered zero boards and saved nothing. The 6-hour crawl only refreshed a short seed list, which is why the live index was a few thousand rows and mostly Lever, SmartRecruiters, and Workable.
2. A restart drops the in-process cron until the next clock tick. There is no external scheduler. The boot crawl covers that gap, and a lock stops a second crawl from starting while one is already running.

`api.smartrecruiters.com/robots.txt` disallows every agent except LinkedInBot, so this change stops calling that API. Accor's own vacancies sitemap is allowed and replaces that board.

A full crawl now deletes rows whose `posted_at` is older than 180 days, and a completed board fetch deletes postings that disappeared from that board. Filled roles no longer sit in `total` forever. Rows between 46 and 180 days can still make `total` larger than `fresh`.
